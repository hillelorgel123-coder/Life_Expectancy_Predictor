The Actuarial Approach

This application abandons simple "years added or subtracted" logic in favor of true actuarial mathematics. It uses the Gompertz-Makeham Law of Mortality to generate a baseline probability of death for each year, applies Proportional Hazards based on underwriting risk factors, and mathematically simulates a population from your current age to age 120 to construct a Survival Curve.

Step 1: Baseline Generation ($q_x$)

Instead of loading massive XML tables into the browser, we use the actuarially respected Gompertz-Makeham function to mathematically approximate the Society of Actuaries (SOA) 2015 Valuation Basic Tables (VBT).

This formula calculates $q_x$, which is the exact probability that a person who has reached age $x$ will die before reaching age $x+1$.

$$ q_x = A + B \cdot c^x $$

Constants (Calibrated for baseline):
A (Makeham Term): Age-independent mortality (accidents).
B & c (Gompertz Term): Exponential age-dependent decline.
Male: A=0.0003, B=0.00004, c=1.095
Female: A=0.0002, B=0.00002, c=1.095

Step 2: Proportional Hazards

To account for your medical profile, we apply a Hazard Ratio. A hazard ratio is a multiplier applied to your baseline $q_x$ every single year of your life.

For example, if you are a current smoker (2.5x hazard), and a healthy 40-year-old has a 0.1% chance of dying this year, your adjusted risk becomes 0.25%.

$$ Adjusted\ q_x = q_x \cdot \prod (Hazard\ Ratios) $$

Step 3: Calculating Survival & Expected Value ($l_x$)

Once we have your $Adjusted\ q_x$ for every year from now until age 120, we simulate a hypothetical population (called $l_x$, starting at 100,000 people). Every year, we subtract the dead ($d_x$) based on your custom probability, leaving the survivors. This generates the Survival Curve shown on your results chart.

The Expected Age of Death is not a guess—it is mathematically derived by taking the integral (Area Under the Curve) of your Survival Curve.
